What this report is

The companion report rates every parameter against the four site types the platform audits. It answers what kind of business is this. It does not answer the question an audit actually has to resolve first: this page, on this site — what is even scoreable here, and what is it worth? A trade-in flow is worth 0.15 of the Conversion section, but only on a trade-in page, and only if the business sells vehicles. Move it one page over and it is worth nothing at all.

So this report crosses the two axes. Four site types, each with a six-page crawl plan, gives twenty-four columns; 145 weighted parameters give the rows. Every cell is that parameter's share of that page's final score — not its in-section weight, but what it actually controls once the page tilt, the site profile and the per-parameter importance ratio have all been applied.

Nothing here is a fresh judgement call. The section tilt is read from singleAuditWorker.js, the site profile and applicability from siteTypeProfiles.js, and the importance ratios from parameterImportance.js. The in-section weights and page gates are transcribed from the eight metric services, with the source line printed against each block in the master matrix. If a number here is wrong, the engine is wrong the same way — which is the point of computing it rather than writing it.
§

How a cell is computed

Three multiplications and a renormalisation stand between a parameter and its influence on a score.

1 · Page tilt

What kind of page is this? A finance page gives Security 22 of its 100; a booking page gives Conversion 25. Eighteen page types, one row each.

2 · Site profile

What kind of business runs it? Applied as a ratio against the neutral row, so a franchise dealer's finance page keeps its Security tilt and a repair shop's contact page never inherits one.

3 · Parameter ratio

Franchise is the reference column, so a franchise dealer scores exactly as it did before the ratings existed. Every other type moves relative to it.

Then the section renormalises: Σ(score × w) / Σ(w) over the parameters that actually apply. That last step is what makes an N/A parameter harmless — it leaves the denominator instead of entering it as a zero.

Four sections do not use that formula, and it changes how you read their rows.
Technical Performance reports PageSpeed's own Lighthouse category score as its headline — fixed weights (LCP 25, TBT 30, CLS 25, FCP 10, SI 10) that no site profile can move. The twelve-component list, the delivery hygiene checks and Sold vehicle feed Graded_Percentage, a diagnostic the overall score never reads.
Security / Compliance deducts from 100 rather than averaging, and the site tilt is rescaled to preserve the original weight sum — so a garage's controls redistribute without the section quietly becoming easier.
Accessibility weights by severity (×3 critical, ×2 serious, ×1 moderate) and folds four keyboard checks into one composite with fixed internal weights.
AEO carries three page-gated parameters — FAQ blocks, E-E-A-T and sameAs — that are simply absent elsewhere.

The five crawl plans

A full-site run visits at most six pages, each one a full Chromium render plus an eight-pillar pass. The page list is therefore both the biggest lever on cost and — because the page decides what is scoreable at all — the biggest lever on what the report can say. These lists are ordered by what each page uniquely unlocks.

The short run: a four-page garage

Home, Services and About exist; Booking, Pricing and Content do not. The run reports 3 / 6 and that is the honest answer. The question worth asking is what the missing three slots would actually have added — and computing it gives a more reassuring result than the headline 3 / 6 suggests.

Reachable in the short run
Home→Services→About

Home carries Appointment booking — the gate was widened to home and generic for servicing sites precisely because a garage's booking form usually sits on the home page. Services carries Pricing transparency, Incentives, FAQ blocks and E-E-A-T. About carries sameAs, E-E-A-T and Form presence.

Weighted parameters lost entirely

None. Every weighted parameter the full six-page plan can reach is also reachable from Home, Services and About. The three servicing page gates that matter — booking, pricing/FAQ, and E-E-A-T/sameAs — each open on more than one page type, and the short run holds at least one of each.

The practical read: a short run costs confidence, not coverage. Every parameter still gets a score; what changes is the quality of the evidence behind it and the number of samples the page average is built from. Pricing transparency is judged off the services page rather than a dedicated price list. Appointment booking is judged off whatever the home page exposes rather than a real scheduler. And the Content page — the highest On-Page SEO and AEO share of any page in any plan, at 19.4 and 15.2 — is simply not there to demonstrate topical authority. That is a narrower and more defensible claim than "3 / 6 of the audit is missing", and it is the one the numbers support.

Where the config and the engine disagree

Six things surfaced when this matrix was first computed from the shipped code. Four have since been closed in the engine; two remain, and both are deliberate. None of them ever broke a score — each one meant a rating in parameterImportance.js was doing less than it read as doing.

Open · by design

The thirteen Technical Performance ratings still cannot move the section score

The headline OverAll() consumes is Percentage: PageSpeed's own Lighthouse category score, computed by Google at fixed weights that no site profile can touch. That is a product decision, not an oversight — the headline stays directly comparable to a PSI run the customer can repeat themselves. The tilt does reach the two derived numbers built from the same components, Delivery_Hygiene and Graded_Percentage, both of which the report shows.

Closed as documented rather than moved. The block now opens with an explicit warning in parameterImportance.js, so the ratings no longer read as load-bearing. Where the site type genuinely changes what Technical Performance is worth is its section weight in siteTypeProfiles.js — 18 for a dealer, 16 for a garage.

Open · a product call, and a small one

Appointment booking is still unreachable in both dealer plans

Neither dealer plan fetches a service page, and that is the only page where a real scheduler lives. Widening the gate to the dealer home page was considered and rejected: checkAppointmentBooking grades a genuine scheduler widget, so a home-page nav link would score at the weak end and read as a failure the site doesn't have.

It is rated Important for a franchise store and Recommended for an independent lot — the smallest of the three original plan gaps, and the residual is one parameter rather than three. Unlocking it means spending a slot on Service instead of Lease or Trade-in, which is a product call.

Closed · the finding that changed a score

Two of the three unreachable dealer gates now open on pages the plan already fetches

Incentives displayed is rated Most Important for a franchise dealer on the reasoning that OEM incentives are its time-bound offer surface — but its gate was service / specials and the franchise plan's sixth slot goes to Lease, so the highest-rated Conversion parameter for that site type went unscored on every franchise audit. The gate now includes lease: a page advertising manufacturer lease offers is an incentive surface, so this is a correction rather than a workaround.

E-E-A-T composite, at 0.10 the second-heaviest AEO parameter, gated on about / content / service — none of which either dealer plan fetches. It now also opens on home, which carries the same evidence the check looks for: who we are, how long we have traded, named staff, review counts.

Together these took franchise coverage from 135 reachable parameters to 137, and both dealer plans from three and two plan gaps respectively down to one each.

Closed · 4 ratings now live

The keyboard sub-checks are multiplied inside the composite

Focus order, Focusable content, Tab index and Aria hidden focus are folded into the Keyboard navigation composite at 8 / 6 / 5 / 3, and the section only ever sees the composite. importance() is now applied inside checkKeyboardNavigation rather than in the section roll-up, so their ratings — including the deliberate drop to Recommended on repair sites, whose "forms" are usually a phone number — reach the score.

Closed · one table

The duplicated page-weight table is gone

singleAuditWorker.js keyed its copy trade / specials while utils/sectionWeights.js keyed the same rows tradein / offers, each matching its own classifier, with a "keep BOTH tables in sync" comment in each file. There is now one table, read through weightsForPageType(), which resolves either vocabulary. Fixing it also surfaced a real bug: the subset-audit path was applying the page tilt without the site profile, so a partial audit of a repair site was weighted as if it were a dealer's.

Open question · not ours to close

Content relevance is rated Most Important for service and repair, and switched off

Hidden on 2026-07-07 by product decision pending a redesign: checkContentRelevance still runs, its 0.06 is commented out of the weights, and its card is hidden in On_Page_SEO.jsx. Its rating is 2233 — a garage has to explain a mechanical problem to a non-mechanic before it can sell the fix, while dealer copy is specification data that is read rather than comprehended. It remains the single highest-value re-enable available, and it is worth the most to the two types it currently does nothing for. The rating is kept, flagged in place, and deliberately not acted on: reversing a stated product decision is not a correctness fix.

Belt and braces · kept

The site-level inventory gate is defence in depth, not a live fix, on the worker path

siteTypeProfiles.js justifies the site gate with a repair shop's /finance-options page classifying as finance and running the whole credit-application battery. On the worker path that cannot happen: a servicing site is classified with classifyServicePageType, which has no finance bucket, so the page lands in generic. The gate is still correct and still worth keeping — any caller that reaches a metric service with the dealer classifier gets the protection — but the specific scenario in the comment is already closed upstream.
