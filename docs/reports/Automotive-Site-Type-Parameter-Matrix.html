Part 1How to read this report

Every parameter the engine implements is rated against each of the four automotive website types on a three-level scale. A rating is not a statement about how good a parameter is — it is a statement about how much that parameter moves the outcome for that kind of business.

✔✔✔ Most ImportantFix first. Either a hard gate, or the parameter that carries this site type's primary revenue mechanism. Failing it costs measurable business.
✔✔ ImportantMaterially weighted. Meaningfully affects ranking, conversion or compliance for this type, but is not the thing the site lives or dies on.
✔ RecommendedMeasure it and fix it when convenient. Real but low leverage for this type — usually diagnostic or polish.
– Not applicableThis site type has no page that carries the parameter. It is dropped from the denominator, never scored zero.

The N/A rule is the most important one here. A parameter that does not apply must be renormalised out of the score, not counted as a failure. A repair shop with no vehicle inventory scoring zero on Vehicle Image Gallery, Trade-In Flow and VDP Content Uniqueness is the single largest source of false-low scores in any audit product — and today all four of these site types resolve to the same internal siteType: "dealer", so that is exactly what happens.

●The four types, as used in this report

“Car dealership” and “automotive dealer” are used interchangeably in everyday language. To make two separate columns meaningful they are split here on the one distinction that actually changes the parameter set: franchise versus independent. If you read them as synonyms, columns 1 and 2 collapse — and the 22 parameters where they differ are precisely the franchise-versus-independent differences.

Column 1

Car Dealership — franchise / OEM new-car store

Manufacturer-appointed dealer. New, used and certified pre-owned inventory on one site.

  • 5,000–50,000 URLs; thousands of VDPs, faceted SRPs
  • Lease specials and advertised APR → Reg-Z / Reg-M exposure
  • Credit application, payment calculator, trade valuation
  • Full service department and parts counter
  • Dealer.com / DealerOn / Dealer Inspire / CDK platforms
Column 2

Automotive Dealer — independent / used-car lot

No manufacturer franchise. Mixed-brand used inventory, often feed-supplied.

  • 50–500 VDPs; far smaller crawl surface
  • No OEM incentives or lease programmes; subprime and buy-here-pay-here finance
  • FTC CARS Rule is the primary compliance exposure
  • Usually no service department
  • No brand halo → reviews, history reports and real photos do the trust work
Column 3

Automotive Service — service centre or chain

Servicing, tyres, fast-fit, dealership service microsites. No vehicle sales.

  • No inventory at all → a whole block of parameters is N/A
  • The funnel is a booking scheduler, not a lead form
  • Service menu, published pricing, coupons and seasonal offers
  • Multi-location → local landing pages, NAP, Google Business Profile
  • Repeat-customer business; loyalty and reminders matter
Column 4

Mechanical / Vehicle Repair — garage, body shop

Independent mechanic, collision repair, specialist or mobile mechanic.

  • 10–40 pages, single location, frequently self-built
  • The conversion is a phone call — click-to-call is the funnel
  • Trust is technician credentials and accreditation, not brand
  • Demand is symptom-driven and answered increasingly by AI, not links
  • Highest incidence of basic hygiene failures: HTTPS, mobile, accessibility

Part 2What the matrix actually says

164implemented parameters rated

  1. 60% of the parameter set does not care what kind of automotive business it is. 98 of 164 parameters rate Important or Most Important for all four types — Core Web Vitals, HTTPS, certificate validity, reputation, titles, H1, contrast, mobile experience, CTA clarity, schema validity, server-rendered content, AI bot access. 63 of them carry an identical rating in all four columns. The shared core is the product; the site type is a tilt on top of it.
  2. Only 23 parameters swing by two levels or more, and 12 of those are pure applicability. 78 more differ by exactly one level — a tilt, not a different job. Strip out the N/A cases and just 11 parameters genuinely change character between site types: title and description uniqueness, canonicals, internal linking (all four are the same scale problem), CSP, Reg-Z/Reg-M disclaimers, breadcrumbs, CTA crowding, AI-friendly linking, and appointment booking — the only one that runs the other way, from Important on a franchise site to Most Important on a garage.
  3. 12 parameters are genuinely inapplicable somewhere, and every one of them is inventory-scoped. Sold-vehicle handling, VDP uniqueness, SRP index control, SRP→VDP links, inventory filtering, no-results UX, vehicle gallery, vehicle history, trade-in flow, financing flow, finance calculator and finance-form security exist only where vehicles are sold. On a service or repair site they must be renormalised out, not scored zero. This is the whole ball game — scoring these as failures would cost a garage roughly a section and a half of its total score.
  4. The differences concentrate in three sections. Conversion & Lead Flow (26 of 33 rows differ), Security & Compliance (15 of 25) and On-Page SEO (13 of 22). Technical Performance (7 of 15) and Accessibility (10 of 24) are nearly flat, because physics and WCAG do not care about business model.
  5. Franchise versus independent is a smaller distinction than either is from service. Columns 1 and 2 differ on 22 parameters; service and repair differ on 30; but 75 parameters separate the two selling types from the two servicing types. If you only ever build two profiles, build sells vehicles and services vehicles.
  6. Compliance is the sharpest split, and it is not about site quality. Anyone taking a credit application is handling SSNs under the GLBA Safeguards Rule; anyone advertising APR or a lease payment is under Reg-Z / Reg-M; independent lots are the FTC CARS Rule's primary target. A garage advertising a $99 brake service carries almost none of that load — which is why Security & Compliance should weigh 13% on a dealer site and 8–9% on a repair site.
  7. AEO inverts the usual hierarchy. Service and repair rate higher than dealers on answer-first structure, FAQ blocks, topical authority, E-E-A-T and per-engine platform scores. Their customers ask questions AI engines now answer directly, and a local business with no brand equity gains the most from being the cited source. Dealers gain from schema and index coverage — being present as structured inventory — more than from being quoted.
  8. Trust inverts too. Testimonials, trust badges, certifications, vehicle history reports and partner logos all rate higher for independents, service and repair than for franchise dealers. A franchise store borrows the manufacturer's credibility; nobody else can.
  9. The engine cannot currently act on any of this. siteTypeDetector returns only dealer, corporate or unknown. All four types collapse to dealer, so a garage is scored against a franchise dealer's expectations. Seven concrete coverage gaps follow in Part 6.

●The six drivers behind every rating

No cell in the matrix is a matter of taste. Every rating that differs between columns traces to one of six structural differences between the businesses. The driver codes appear against each row in Part 5.

D1Page inventory

Does the site actually contain the page type that carries this parameter? A VDP-scoped check cannot matter on a site with no VDPs — it is N/A, not low-priority. This is the single biggest source of difference between the four columns.

D2Conversion mechanism

How a lead is actually captured. Franchise and independent dealers convert through forms (ePrice, trade valuation, credit app). Service converts through a booking scheduler. Repair converts through a phone call. Whatever carries the lead gets the weight.

D3Regulatory exposure

Advertised finance/lease terms and collected PII. Reg-Z / Reg-M / FTC CARS Rule / GLBA Safeguards apply to anyone advertising APR or taking a credit application; a repair shop advertising a $99 brake service carries almost none of that load.

D4Site scale

Crawl budget, index bloat, duplicate content and template consistency are functions of URL count. A 40,000-URL franchise site and a 15-page garage site have opposite failure modes — one drowns in near-duplicates, the other has too little to index.

D5Trust deficit

A franchise dealer borrows credibility from the OEM badge. An independent lot, a service chain and a garage have no such halo, so reviews, credentials, real photos and transparent pricing have to do that work directly.

D6Query intent in AI answers

Dealers are found through transactional inventory queries ('2022 RAV4 under $30k near me'). Service and repair are found through informational and symptom queries ('why is my car shaking', 'timing belt cost') — exactly the queries AI answer engines answer instead of ranking links for.

ATechnical Performance

Almost flat across all four types — Core Web Vitals are a universal ranking and bounce signal and nobody escapes them. The differences are entirely about payload shape and page churn: inventory sites carry 30–50 image galleries and heavy third-party tag stacks (D4), repair sites carry DIY page-builder bloat, and only inventory sites have URLs that die.

The section where service and repair systematically outrank dealers in importance. Their customers ask questions that AI answer engines now answer directly, and a local business with no brand equity gains the most from being the cited source. Dealers gain from schema and index coverage — being present as structured inventory — more than from being quoted.

Part 6Where the engine cannot yet act on this

Seven places where what this matrix says a site type needs and what Backend/metricServices/ actually measures diverge. Ordered by how much score distortion each one causes today.

Pricing transparency is VDP/Offers/Lease/Finance-scoped Service, Repair

checkPricingTransparency only runs when the page classifies as vdp, specials, lease or finance. Published service pricing is arguably the strongest differentiator on a service or repair site and is currently never measured there.

Fix → Extend the page-type gate to include service, and score 'is a price or price range published for each named service'.

Appointment booking is service-page-scoped only Repair

checkAppointmentBooking runs only when classifyPageType returns 'service'. A repair shop's booking form frequently sits on the home page or a /book page that classifies as generic, so the single most important conversion parameter for that site type goes unscored.

Fix → Run the booking check on home and generic pages when the site type is service or repair.

No site-type sub-classification All four

siteTypeDetector returns only dealer / corporate / unknown. All four of the types in this report collapse into 'dealer', so a garage is scored against a franchise dealer's expectations — including inventory, trade-in and finance parameters that it correctly does not have.

Fix → Add a sub-type resolver (franchise / independent / service / repair) and key a parameter-applicability profile off it, the same way SECTION_PAGE_WEIGHTS is keyed off page type today.

Local SEO signals largely unimplemented Service, Repair (most), Dealer (independent)

NAP consistency exists only inside AEO's citationConsistency signal. Google Business Profile linkage, location-page completeness, local landing pages and review signals are not measured at all — and they are the dominant discovery surface for exactly the two site types that need them most.

Fix → Add a Local SEO parameter group scored only for service, repair and independent-dealer site types.

Service / AutoRepair schema not type-validated Service, Repair

Structured-data validation covers Organization, LocalBusiness, Product, Offer, Vehicle, FAQPage, BreadcrumbList, Article and Review. AutoRepair, AutomotiveBusiness and Service are not in the required-field validator, so a repair site marked up correctly and a repair site marked up as a generic LocalBusiness score the same.

Fix → Add AutoRepair / AutoPartsStore / Service to the per-@type required-property validator, and flag AutoDealer markup on a site with no inventory as a type mismatch.

Certifications & awards is a generic check Service, Repair

checkCertificationsAwards looks for award-like language generically. For service and repair the specific accreditations (ASE, I-CAR, NAPA AutoCare, Bosch Service, RAA, MTA, VACC, RACV) are the credibility currency and deserve a named list.

Fix → Add a trade-accreditation dictionary keyed by market, reusing the country config already added in Frontend/src/config/countries.js.

Call tracking not distinguished from conversion tracking Service, Repair

checkConversionTracking looks for form-submit and generic events. Where the phone is the funnel, the meaningful check is whether a call-tracking number or a tel: click event is wired to analytics.

Fix → Split a call-tracking sub-check out of Conversion_Tracking and weight it up for service and repair site types.

●How to wire this in

  1. Add a site sub-type to the detector. dealershipDetector.js already collects the signals needed to separate these — inventory and VIN presence separates dealer from service; OEM-franchise language, lease programmes and a manufacturer logo separate franchise from independent; a booking scheduler with no inventory separates service from repair. Return siteSubType: 'franchise' | 'independent' | 'service' | 'repair' alongside the existing siteType, keeping the current fail-open behaviour when signals are inconclusive.
  2. Encode applicability, not just weight. Add an appliesTo[siteSubType] flag to each parameter beside the existing per-page-type gating. The renormalising roll-up already in place then removes N/A parameters from the denominator for free — no scoring change needed.
  3. Key a site-type section profile off it, multiplying into SECTION_PAGE_WEIGHTS rather than replacing it.
  4. Adjust page discovery expectations. pageDiscovery.js currently looks for SRP and VDP pages on every site. On a service or repair sub-type it should look for service-menu, booking, location and pricing pages instead, and not report missing inventory as a finding.
  5. Do the three cheap coverage fixes first — pricing transparency on service pages, booking detection outside /service, and AutoRepair schema validation. Between them they cover the three highest-rated parameters that a service or repair site currently cannot score on at all.
